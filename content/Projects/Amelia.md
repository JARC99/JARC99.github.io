I always felt that the whole point of studying engineering was to to learn how to bring one's ideas to the real world. From the earliest days of my studies I tried, often unsuccesfully, to apply what we learned in class in something I could hold on my hands. With this in mind, when I learned that my faculty's SAE Aero Design team was opening applications I jumped on to the opportunity to join. I was  in my third semester and took part in the selection process as a part of a group of five applicants, from my group, only three were selected, I was not within those. Back then I felt kinda frustrated with the result, I still don't fully know what went wrong, all 5 of us took part in building the marshmellow tower we were given as a test of our engineering skills, I guess they couldn't accept everyone and I may have not shown any outstandign skills. That 2019 competition was hosted in our campus so at least I got to see some aircraft from other universities when I attended the technical presentation day.

I ended up joining the team about a year later by means of invitation of a friend of mine that had joined the team recently. During that first season I tried to catch up with the knowledged that my peers had alredy accumulated the year before and even volunteered to write the technical report. The original plan was to compete in the regular class in the mexican edition of the competition and also in the micro class in the SAE East in the USA. Suffice to say that I did not enjoy the expeience that much. As the one in charge of documenting the whole design and fabrication process the lack of organization around both the aircraft and the team became painfully evident. As someone who hates to not keep deadlines and plans his work accordingly, I had a hard time dealing with the lack of commitment of some of the people that were supossed to be responsible of leading important parts of the project. No wonder, I use to think to myself, this team has not been able to complete a single flight round in its by now 4 years of existence. The pandemic ended up throwing a spanner into the works and a the competition originally planned for march ended up taking place as a online-only event in october. On the flipside, the team got its best result ever: 6th place overall and 4th place in the technical report category; mostly a product of the lack of flight rounds, as we didn't event have a complete aircraft by then.

After this not so enjoyable experience I distanced myself from the team activities. I was planning on spending a year abroad and my graduation project was also comming up so I had convinced myself that I had better things to do that to work on lost cause projects. The fact that no 2021 season took place due to sanitary reasons probably did not help as well. By late summer of 2021, around the time the new rules are published for next year's season my partcipation in SAE competitions was all but a not so fun annecdote of my college years, or so I thought.

I knew Eber Maciel from some classes we had attended together during our junior year. I knew he was a brilliant and very competent guy which is something  I can always respect. We finally had the chance of working together once I eventually join Dr. Amezquita's research group for a summer research programm that the university used to offer for students. We ended up competing (and winning) that year's mexican edition of the MATLAB minidrone competion also during that time, admitedly Eber did most of the work that time, but it was a fun thing to do nevertheless. 

It is with this background that he came to me in late july 2026 and asked me to help him out in the new SAE Aero Design season as he had been made co-captain (to try to avoid some ego issues back in the day, this team had the quirck of always having two co-captians). He and Giovanni Lazo, the other co-captain, had been in the team since that 2019 season I had not been accepted, they had now been given full reign over the team and had the full intention of finally achieving something of note. 

I thought about it a little bit and reluctanly agreed, not before setting out a list of demands regarding the organization of the team and the way the project was to be managed. These suggestions were pretty much in line with what Eber and Giovanni had thought tehmselves were the main weaknesses of the team so we got to work right away. 

We reduced a mostly innactive 50 team member list to a group of around 25 people who were fully commited to the cause, half of them of classes below ours, we knew that this was our last competition and we wanted to mke sure that the team had a future after us. I tried to teach the little I thought I knew about design to the new kids and we set out a project plan based on [NASA's System Engineering Handbook](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL23710690M/NASA_systems_engineering_handbook).
