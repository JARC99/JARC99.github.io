Growing up in a small semi-rural town in southern Mexico there where not a lot of airsports venues laying around in the vicinity. My family wasn't precissely one with a rich history in the field of aeronautics either, so my first concious interaction with a flying object was a kite.

The first episode I remember clearly was with my uncle I don't remembe well if he built or bought some kites and we flew them from my grandmothers backyard. Seing how something previouyl earth-bound could go so high was, to put it mildly a mesmerizing experience. 

Back in my elementary school years I always had trouble making friends so my mother would sometimes invitite kids from nearby houses to the house so maybe I could develop at least some social skills. There was this kid named Ezequiel who came to the house a couple of times and we would work on building kites. "We" is admitedly an overstatement as although I did helped here and there I don't really remember ever taking a very active role in the process, I would mostly limit myself to observe the work Ezequiel did until time ran out and he had to go home. I don't rememberif we ever flew those kites but I must admit the impression did leave a mark on me as some years later I would attempt (unsuccesfully) to build a delta kite of my own replicating a store bought one we had lost the on the beach during the previous holidays.

I guess you could say I still had a pending round with kite making since then. By know I had been studying aeronatical engineering for some years now so the whole issue had also become a matter of professinonal pride. 

With this spirit during the winter holidays of 2024 I set out to build a kite in the hexgonal design I remembered from my childhood. Traditionally, kites were built with a frame made out of thin sticks extracted from the leaflets of dried coconut palm leaves, string and tissue paper glued on top. As I know lived in Monterrey, I needed to use more "modern" (i.e. accesibel for city folk) materials so I ended up settling for some thin pine skewers I found in a craft shop.

As one does I look for some information online of which I found some good references. I mostly followed [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CxR630eFLxs) while making some adjustments given the materials I had at hand. Once it was done, my sister accompanied me to an empty lot so give it a try. If I may say so it myself, it flew pretty well and all my little mistakes during assembly I was so concerned about were not even visible once the thing was 20 m in the air. Here is a nice picture:

Some months later while vacationing in y home state of Chiapas shortly before leaving for Germany to start my gradute studies I decided to cross another item from my bucket list and finally build one of those romboid kites you always see in North American cartoons and movies. I later learned that these are called Eddy kites and with the help of [Tim Parish's aweseme website](https://www.my-best-kite.com/) I was able to built one even with the limited amount of time and supplies I hd at hand. This one did not fly as good as the previous one, probably due to the heavier frame and sail made out of a trash bag instead of light tissue paper, nevertheless me and my brother managed to get it on the air and take some ok photos of it floating above the mangroves.

I think I can now look back at my child self and confidently tell him that we did learn at least something from all the people that built and flew kites with us and that we know we can say that we actually can built a kite. I still have so many othe rkite related projects I'd like to try, but that will need to wait until I have some more free time.
