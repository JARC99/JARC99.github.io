Briefly before starting my bachelor's studies in aeronautical engineering I had set myself the personal goal of making something fly. For me it made no sense to earn a degree that said I knew how flying stuff worked if I had never made anything that flew with my own two hands. 

Now, as anyone who has ever asked who invented the airplane when a contrarian or a Brazilian is present is painfully aware, there are many ways of defining what counts as fly and although I never drew up an official RFP, I am pretty sure I meant powered, controlled flight. 

I do recall starting to work on this around the end of the first semester out of the little room I rented. I distinctly remember storing my half finished wings made out of the cardboard of a a generic brand cereal box. I had made (what I thought was)  a thorough literature review on YouTube, had ordered all the needed components out of a heterogeneous collection of Chinese vendors, getting a doubly charged in one of them (thank you Paypal for giving me my money back), and had finally put it all together into something that looked liked an airplane. I even sculpted a small fuselage to carry that yellow 3S LiPo I was utterly terrified of. 

It is important to note that I had never done anything like that, that is either build or **fly** an remote controlled aircraft. At most I had flown some kites as a child but you don't really need to be that talented to hold a string while the wind holds you aircraft aloft. Nevertheless full of the confidence only blissful ignorance can give you, I crossed the train tracks that ran along the avenue in front of my house and set out to the nearest park.

As my airfield I choose a pair of empty basketball courts surrounded by trees and overhead wires. This would have been limited space for a 3 in drone, let alone a supposed "glider" with a wingspan above 1 m. Following the tutorials I had seen online I held my aircraft firmly, armed the motor and gave it full throttle before launching it into the air. The subsequent seconds were thankfully captured in video and can bee seen at the start of this fun compilation:

![](https://youtu.be/SXQ2YIHrVqE)

Thankfully I had the foresight of recording this first ever flight of mine, I seemed to have known back then that I would want to look back at this epic first flight of mine. The rest of the video shows a subset of the other aircraft I managed to destroy during my first years of college, some others I sadly did not capture on video, all fond memories of how not to do stuff.

I eventually managed to get a couple of things in the air reliably before finishing my degree, something I am way more proud of than any exam grade I ever achieved. I still wouldn't call me a great aeronaut, but I guess that will always be a work in progress.
